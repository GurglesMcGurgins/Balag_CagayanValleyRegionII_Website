History

A Brief History

From prehistoric caves to a modern region

Cagayan Valley is one of the oldest inhabited places in the Philippines. Fossils of Homo luzonensis found in Callao Cave, Peñablanca, date back more than 50,000 years. Long before colonizers arrived, the valley was home to indigenous groups such as the Ibanag, Itawes, Gaddang, Yogad, Isinai, and Agta, while the Ivatan people settled the islands of Batanes. They lived along the Cagayan River, farming, fishing, and trading with neighboring peoples.

Spanish explorer Juan de Salcedo reached the mouth of the Cagayan River in 1572, and in 1581 Juan Pablo Carreón founded Nueva Segovia (present-day Lal-lo), which became the seat of one of the earliest dioceses in the country. Dominican friars spread Christianity and built the brick churches that still stand today. In the late 1700s the Spanish tobacco monopoly turned the valley into the country's leading tobacco producer, a legacy that continues in Isabela and Cagayan.

Over time the region was divided into provinces: Nueva Vizcaya (1839), Isabela (1856, named after Queen Isabella II of Spain), Batanes, and later Quirino. During World War II, the Battle of Balete Pass in Nueva Vizcaya was one of the fiercest battles of the liberation of Luzon in 1945. In 1972 the five provinces were formally grouped as Region II, with Tuguegarao City as its regional center.

01 Festivals 6 festivals
  • Pavvurulun Afi FestivalTuguegarao City's fire-themed festival held every August
  • Aggao Nac Cagayanthe founding anniversary celebration of Cagayan Province every June
  • Bambanti FestivalIsabela's scarecrow festival honoring its farmers every January
  • Ammungan FestivalNueva Vizcaya's "gathering" of its many tribes and cultures
  • Panagdadapun FestivalQuirino's celebration of unity among its ethnic groups
  • Batanes Daythe Ivatan founding celebration with traditional songs and dances
02 Traditional Crafts 5 crafts
  • Vakulthe Ivatan headgear woven from voyavoy palm fibers to shield against sun and rain
  • Ivatan stone housesthick limestone and coral walls with cogon-grass roofs built to survive typhoons
  • Gaddang beadworkcolorful beaded ornaments and woven garments of Isabela and Nueva Vizcaya
  • Inabel weavinghand-loomed cotton textiles carried by Ilocano settlers in the valley
  • Rattan and bamboo craftsbaskets and furniture made in the towns of Quirino
03 Traditional Foods 5 dishes
  • Pancit Batil PatongTuguegarao noodles topped with egg, meat, and a side of egg-drop soup
  • Pancit CabaganIsabela's saucy noodles with quail eggs and lechon carajay
  • Tuguegarao Longganisashort, garlicky Ybanag sausage
  • Moriecos and Inatatasticky rice cakes with coconut and brown sugar
  • Uvod and LunyisIvatan banana-pith balls and salted cured pork
